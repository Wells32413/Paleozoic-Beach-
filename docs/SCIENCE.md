# Scientific scope and content policy

The launch site is a fictional present-day Illinois facility in the general Mazon Creek/Braidwood region. Its time engine changes time, not geographic coordinates. The first reconstruction is approximately 309 Ma, Pennsylvanian, in the coastal Illinois Basin. It is a turbid coastal estuary/nursery interpretation, not a generic open-ocean reef. A precise drill-site coordinate, stratigraphic horizon, and paleogeographic reconstruction remain to be selected with expert review; the slice does not implement GIS or moving plate coordinates.

Bandringa is associated with Pennsylvanian deposits including Mazon Creek. Sallan & Coates (2014), *The long-rostrumed elasmobranch Bandringa …*, Journal of Vertebrate Paleontology 34(1):1–18, describes anatomy and a proposed coastal nursery/migration interpretation. This supports a cautious coastal setting rather than treating all organisms from the broader deposit as a single marine community. Bibliographic lead: https://doi.org/10.1080/02724634.2013.782875 . Reference content and exact numerical age bounds must be checked in primary literature before a science-approved release; no online literature retrieval succeeded here.

The catalog labels the enabled animal Bandringa rayi, not a confirmed full-color reconstruction. Its long rostrum is supported by fossils. Its movement, soft tissues, skin, coloration, dimensions and depth range in this slice are illustrative. Generated body/fin blocks are a placeholder, not an anatomically validated model. The slow bottom-oriented swimming path is a behavior prototype, not measured paleobiology. There are no automatic predator attacks.

Cladoselache, Stethacanthus and Edestus giganteus are recorded but disabled. Their catalog date ranges are broad placeholders requiring taxon-level review, cited occurrences, regional verification and habitat validation. Do not use them as authoritative occurrence data. Cladoselache cannot be added to the Pennsylvanian slice merely to increase variety. Edestus and Stethacanthus need independent occurrence evidence even where broad geological intervals overlap.

Before adding a spawnable species, require primary sources, approved time bounds, regional occurrence, environment, anatomy references, locomotion assumptions, diet and uncertainty labels. Current eligibility uses time bounds plus an exact habitat string; replace this deliberately simple prototype gate with region and stratigraphy identifiers before introducing expedition selection. Keep claims and reconstruction assumptions distinct in the discovery UI.
